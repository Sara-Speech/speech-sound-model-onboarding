Proposal for SLP review

Quick Sound Check

A proposed 2–3 minute picture-naming screen that parents run at home. It aims to show which consonants a child has difficulty with, in which word positions, and at which level (singleton, cluster, vocalic /r/). It points parents to practice and, when warranted, to an SLP.

Why a quick screen

Parents often notice that their child's speech "sounds off" but don't know which sound is involved, whether it is typical for the child's age, or where to start. A full articulation assessment answers these questions but is usually 15–25 minutes or more, and access often involves a waitlist.

Sara's app already offers home practice organized the way SLPs structure treatment: target sound → word position (initial, medial, final) → clusters, and vocalic /r/ for R. What is missing is a short entry point that places a child at a sensible starting level.

Design goals

  • Screen all 23 consonants, then narrow to word position, then to clusters and vocalic /r/ where relevant.
  • Keep child time to 2–3 minutes.
  • Interpret errors against developmental norms, so typical developmental errors are not presented as problems.
  • Report to parents in plain language with examples, and suggest an SLP evaluation when findings warrant one.
  • Remain a screen. It does not diagnose, rate severity, or replace an evaluation.

Clinical basis

The procedure follows standard articulation testing practice, made shorter by targeting several sounds per word and adding probe items only where needed.

Procedure

The child names a picture on a phone or tablet while a parent sits with them. If the child doesn't know the word, the parent taps "say it with me" and the app models the word for the child to repeat. Each production is recorded and automatically transcribed into IPA. Only the target consonants in each word are scored.

  1. Set-up (about 10 seconds)

    The parent enters the child's age and tests the microphone. The age changes how results are interpreted, not which words are given.

  2. Round 1 · Screen (30 words, about 2 minutes)

    Each word targets 1–4 consonants. Together the words sample every consonant in initial and final position (where these occur in English). Later-developing sounds (/ɹ s z l θ ð ʃ ʧ ʤ v/) are also sampled in medial position. One S-cluster, one L-cluster and one R-cluster word are included, plus five words with vocalic /r/ (/ɝ/). Early-developing sounds come first as warm-up.

  3. Round 2 · Confirm (only where an error occurred)

    Any sound and position with an error and fewer than 3 productions gets 1–2 more words, for 3 productions total. This separates consistent errors from one-off slips and transcription mistakes.

  4. Round 3 · Find the level (clusters and vocalic /r/)

    If a cluster word was in error but the singleton is correct, 4 cluster words find which clusters are affected (e.g., /tɹ/ vs /fɹ/; /sp/ vs /st/). If the singleton is also in error, clusters are not probed and practice starts with the singleton. If vocalic /r/ was in error, 5 words find which vowel contexts are affected (/ɑɹ ɔɹ ɝ ɪɹ ɛɹ/).

  5. Parent report (immediate)

    For each sound with confirmed errors: the positions and levels affected, examples of what was heard, an age-based interpretation, any pattern found, and a suggested first practice level. A recommendation to see an SLP appears when the referral rules are met.

Limits on length

  • No more than 2 extra words per sound × position. No more than 3 productions per position outside Round 3.
  • If the child names the picture with a different word ("bunny" for rabbit), the app models the target once and marks the item as imitated.
  • If a recording can't be scored reliably (noise, unclear production), it is repeated once and then skipped.
  • Hard cap of about 45 items. A child with errors on many sounds is better served by an SLP evaluation than by a longer screen.

Scoring rules

Per production

  • Each target consonant is scored correct or incorrect, based on an automatic broad IPA transcription of the production.
  • Incorrect productions are classified as substitution (with the sound heard), omission, or unclear.
  • Non-target sounds in the word are not scored.

Per sound × position

  • Confirmed error: 2 or more incorrect productions, making up at least 40% of productions.
  • Slip: a single incorrect production not repeated on confirmation. Not reported to parents.
  • Correct: no incorrect productions.

Per sound (what parents see)

  • Practice confirmed error, and the child is at or past the 90% mastery age.
  • Still developing confirmed error, but the child is younger than the mastery age. Recheck in about 6 months.
  • On track no confirmed error.

Referral rules (draft)

The report recommends an SLP evaluation when any of these is true:

  • An early-developing sound (/b n m p h w d/) is in error at age 4 or older.
  • 3 or more sounds are rated Practice.
  • Final consonant deletion occurs in 2 or more words at age 4 or older.
  • Any sound is rated Practice at age 8 or older.

Question for you: Are these thresholds reasonable for a screen? What would you add, remove or change, for example intelligibility concerns, atypical error patterns, or vowel errors?

Word lists

Round 1 words were chosen so that a small number of picturable words covers every needed sound and position. Covering every position once took 28–30 words. Covering every position twice took 60 words, about the length of a full articulation test. That is why Round 1 samples once, and probe items are added only where errors appear.

Selection principles

  • Picturable and familiar to most 3–4 year olds. To be checked against age-of-acquisition norms.
  • No more than 4 scored targets per word, and target sounds not adjacent where avoidable.
  • Clusters are scored as clusters. The /ɹ/ in "toothbrush" counts toward R-clusters, not toward initial or medial R.
  • Medial /t/ and /d/ are avoided, because they are usually flapped in American English (water, ladder).
  • Initial /ð/ uses "that" with a pointing prompt ("I want that one!"), because few picturable nouns begin with /ð/.
  • /ʒ/ is not currently included. It is rare in early vocabulary.

The parent report

What parents see

  • A headline, for example "2 sounds to practice, 1 still developing, 20 on track".
  • One card per sound with confirmed errors, with examples of what was heard ("said /wɛd/ for red").
  • Positions and levels affected: start, middle, end, blends, vowel + R.
  • An age note: "Most children say R clearly by about 6."
  • A suggested starting point in the practice program, e.g., R → single sound → start of words, or R → blends → TR, GR.
  • Patterns in everyday words, with the clinical term in small print.
  • An SLP recommendation when the referral rules are met, and a clear statement that this is a screen, not a diagnosis.

How we plan to validate it

  1. Recorded examples. Check scoring on recorded correct and error productions of every word in the lists.
  2. Pilot with SLP judgment. 40–60 children aged 3–9 complete the screen. An SLP independently scores the same productions from the recordings. We compare agreement for each sound and position, and on the overall recommendation (practice / developing / refer). Target: sensitivity and specificity of at least 0.80 for confirmed errors.
  3. Family beta. Completion time (goal: median under 3 minutes), how well parents understand the report, and agreement with later SLP evaluations for children who were referred.

Question for you: Would you be willing to score pilot recordings, or advise on the protocol? What agreement level would you consider acceptable for a screen?
